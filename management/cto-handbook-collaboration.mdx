## Meetings

A meeting should have a purpose someone can state and an output someone can find afterwards. I would keep the number small and give each one a single job, so that nobody has to work out which meeting a question belongs in.

Two rules from our handbook cover most of it. Every meeting's page holds its purpose, what to bring, the agenda and what comes out of it. And every meeting has an owner who prepares it, leads it and follows up on the decisions.

### How we do it at Mav9

| Meeting | When | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stand-up | Monday, Wednesday and Friday, 25 minutes | Keep everyone aligned on goal progress, next steps and blockers. |
| All hands | Thursday, 60 minutes | Review project progress, share shipped outcomes and company decisions, and resolve shared issues. |
| Monthly Planning | First Monday of the month, 90 minutes | Agree the project's goals and priorities and select the next goals to shape. |

Everyone attends all three.

**Stand-up.** Each person prepares a one-minute update: what moved on the goals they own or support, what they aim to move before the next stand-up, and where they need help or a decision. Goal owners update the expected date beforehand. That takes about fifteen minutes. The remaining ten are an open floor for questions and concerns. Detailed problem-solving goes to a follow-up with the people involved.

**All hands.** Ten minutes of company news from the founders, including customer wins or losses and what they mean for priorities. Fifteen minutes of shipped outcomes, where Owners demo goals that are newly done and recognise the people who helped. Fifteen minutes of milestone updates from each Lead: where it stands, what moved, the expected finish against the target date, and any risks or decisions needed. Then an open floor, and five minutes to assign anything unresolved to a named person with a date. We finish with a round of GeoGuessr.

Monthly Planning does not produce a detailed month of work. It chooses what needs shaping and who will do it. The detailed work happens afterwards between the Sponsor and the Owner, and a goal can start when its Owner has capacity. We got here after three earlier versions; see [We Kept Trying to Plan Everything](/blog/category/management/becoming-a-cto-four-planning-processes-in-a-year).

Stand-up is where the dashboard became useful to the team. It shows goal status in a conversation we were already having.

We also run retros, where people vote on suggestions for improvement. In one, someone wrote “I find planning confusing”, and it got more votes than any other suggestion.

Our handbook's norms for any meeting: show up on time or say you will be late, drop out if it is not relevant to you, and do not schedule one without an agenda and a goal. If no meeting exists for something an Owner needs to keep people informed about, the Owner sets one up.

## Feedback

### Giving

Feedback is most useful early, direct and specific. Our values put it as directness with care over silence, and make conflict about facts, not people. On Slack the norm is to praise publicly and criticise privately.

When someone's decision goes badly, I first ask what they knew, and whether we had given them the context and authority we said we had. Sometimes there is a judgement to improve. Sometimes there is something I failed to provide.

### Receiving

This is the half I control. An engineer challenged my account of what I had communicated about a decision. Proving how many times I had mentioned it would have missed his point, which was that it still was not clear enough to act on. How I respond affects whether he raises the next concern.

Another time, engineers picked up decisions I had left open, and I read their initiative as evidence against me. It was feedback about where I had left something unanswered. See [Imposter Syndrome Is Real](/blog/category/management/becoming-a-cto-imposter-syndrome-is-real).

### How we do it at Mav9

- The Owner and Sponsor roles create a regular place for feedback on the work: the Sponsor accepts and tests the result before it ships.
- Retro suggestions are voted on.
- The frameworks we use for giving feedback, and the performance review meeting, are in [Playbooks](/blog/category/management/cto-handbook-playbooks).

## How the team is organised

At ten people I would organise by responsibility for an outcome. A reporting line would not tell anyone who decides what on a given goal. Titles matter less than knowing who owns a goal, who can change its scope and who has the final say in an area.

### How we do it at Mav9

We are about ten engineers. The roles people hold are attached to goals and milestones: Owner, Sponsor, Supporter and Milestone Lead. These are responsibilities people on the team take on, and the same person can be an Owner on one goal and a Supporter on another. Our first value is “we are all team leads”: the person closest to the work drives it.

The founders agree the next priorities across milestones with the Milestone Leads.

### Without a product manager

We have no product manager. The work a PM would do did not disappear: understanding customer needs, choosing what to pursue, defining outcomes, deciding what to leave out. It sits with the founders, including me, and the Sponsor role is how it reaches each goal.

I do not know yet whether we need a dedicated PM. Two things would make me revisit it: more customer requests than we can triage in a week, or finding that we keep choosing from the existing backlog because nobody has time to find out what is missing. The reasoning is in [Ten Engineers, No Product Manager](/blog/category/management/becoming-a-cto-building-a-team-when-there-isnt-one-yet).

### Changing the structure

We are on our fourth way of planning in a year. The latest rules are still new, and I want to find out how well they hold up before changing them again.
