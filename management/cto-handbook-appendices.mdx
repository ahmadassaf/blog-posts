## Appendix A: A template for shaping a goal

From [Processes](/blog/category/management/cto-handbook-processes). I fill this in with the person doing the work, and add detail only where a real question needs it.

- **Outcome:** Who should be able to do what?
- **Why now:** Why does this deserve attention ahead of the other goals?
- **Evidence:** What would show us that it works, and which assumptions need checking?
- **Owner and Sponsor:** Who owns delivery, and who supplies the context and agrees scope?
- **Boundaries:** What is included, excluded or dependent on other work?
- **Next check:** When will we look at the result and decide what happens next?

## Appendix B: The roles on a goal

From [Group Habits](/blog/category/management/cto-handbook-group-habits) and [Infrastructure](/blog/category/management/cto-handbook-infrastructure).

| Role | How many | What they owe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Milestone Lead | One per milestone | Keeps the milestone outcome clear and on track, makes sure its goals cover it, keeps dependencies current, and updates everyone weekly. |
| Sponsor | One per goal | Explains why the goal matters, brings the customer and product view, approves scope changes, and accepts and tests the result before it ships. |
| Owner | One per goal, a different person from the Sponsor | Shapes the goal with the Sponsor, keeps its description current, makes implementation decisions, coordinates Supporters, and reports progress. |
| Supporter | As needed | Owns an agreed contribution, fetches the context they need, and brings progress and obstacles to the Owner. |

## Appendix C: Product responsibilities without a product manager

From [Collaboration](/blog/category/management/cto-handbook-collaboration). This is the division I drafted for the team to discuss.

**Leadership owes the team:**

- Who each goal is for, what problem it addresses and what finished work looks like.
- One shared order of priorities. When it changes, agreement on what moves out, changes or waits.
- Shared customer notes, and engineers in those conversations.
- Decisions recorded where the work is happening.
- An answer within one working day to a scope question, concern or draft.

**Engineers owe the team:**

- Questions about the scope before building.
- The code connected to the work it belongs to.
- Enough understanding of the main customer journeys to use and explain them.
- The change taken through to pre-production and the affected journey used end to end, including its failure cases.
- For a blocked item: what is missing, who could help, and the next action.

## Appendix D: Questions about a release path

From [Processes](/blog/category/management/cto-handbook-processes). A team should be able to answer these about its own service.

- After a version is released, does it reach each environment automatically, or does a person promote it?
- Who decides that a release goes out: the service owner alone, or with a review?
- How do you roll back in practice: revert the commit, redeploy the previous version, or fix forward?
- When did you last roll something back, and how long did it take?

The exercise I would start with: take one customer journey all the way through, including a failure and recovery, and have another engineer repeat it.

## Appendix F: Reading

Grouped by the chapter each one informed.

### The role

- Camille Fournier, [The Manager's Path](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-managers-path/9781491973882/). The later chapters describe holding direction and the whole picture while the team moves without you.
- Charity Majors, [The Engineer/Manager Pendulum](https://charity.wtf/2017/05/11/the-engineer-manager-pendulum/). On the change in work underneath a change in title.
- Will Larson, [Work on what matters](https://staffeng.com/guides/work-on-what-matters/). For deciding where technical involvement helps most.
- Lara Hogan, [When to delegate, when to say no](https://larahogan.me/blog/when-to-delegate-when-to-say-no/). The exercise I used to sort my commitments.

### Trust, ownership and decisions

- Netflix, [Culture](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture). “Context, not control” is the shortest statement of why ownership has to run both ways.
- Henrik Kniberg, [Spotify engineering culture, part 1](https://blog.crisp.se/2014/03/27/henrikkniberg/spotify-engineering-culture-part-1). High alignment is what makes high autonomy safe.
- Rouan Wilsenach, [Ship / Show / Ask](https://martinfowler.com/articles/ship-show-ask.html). Our vocabulary for when a change needs someone else before it lands.
- Sarah Milstein, [How team agreements promote high performance](https://leaddev.com/culture/how-team-agreements-promote-high-performance). A practical companion to writing a working agreement.

### Planning and product

- Ryan Singer, [Shape Up](https://basecamp.com/shapeup). The chapters on appetite and boundaries are the ones I reread.
- Martin Fowler, [The New Methodology](https://martinfowler.com/articles/newMethodology.html). Adaptive against predictive planning.
- Henrik Kniberg, [Making sense of MVP](https://blog.crisp.se/2016/01/25/henrikkniberg/making-sense-of-mvp). The skateboard-to-car picture, with the reasoning underneath it.
- Marty Cagan, [Product is Hard](https://vimeo.com/333559833). Keeps the PM conversation connected to product value.

### Building with AI

- Simon Willison, [Not all AI-assisted programming is vibe coding (but vibe coding rocks)](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Mar/19/vibe-coding/). On taking responsibility for code you understand.
- Birgitta Böckeler, [The role of developer skills in agentic coding](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/13-role-of-developer-skills.html). The framing, steering and correction involved in working with an agent.
- Addy Osmani, [The 70% problem](https://addyo.substack.com/p/the-70-problem-hard-truths-about). Where AI-assisted building saves time and where it does not.
- DORA, [Balancing AI tensions: moving from adoption to effective use](https://dora.dev/insights/balancing-ai-tensions/). On the verification work that comes with faster generation.

### Review, release and compliance

- Google Engineering Practices, [The Standard of Code Review](https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/standard.html). What a reviewer is actually deciding.
- Martin Fowler, [Patterns for Managing Source Code Branches](https://martinfowler.com/articles/branching-patterns.html). Separates branching choices from review and release choices.
- Steven Shorrock, [The Varieties of Human Work](https://humanisticsystems.com/2016/12/05/the-varieties-of-human-work/). Language for the difference between what a procedure says and what happens.
- Phil Venables, [Control Reliability Engineering](https://www.philvenables.com/post/control-reliability-engineering-cre-applying-sre-principles-to-cybersecurity-controls). Checking whether a control keeps working after its first successful test.
- GitLab, [The importance of a handbook-first approach](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/company/culture/all-remote/handbook-first/). Whether documentation is part of how people work or maintained separately for a review.
