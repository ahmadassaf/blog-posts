Learning in private produces understanding. Learning in public produces understanding plus an artifact: a post, a note, a question, a half-working repo. The artifact is what changes everything, because an artifact can be corrected.

When I keep my learning to myself, the only error-checking available is my own. I can reread my notes a hundred times and never notice the flaw in them, because the same brain that made the mistake is the one reviewing it. The moment I publish the same material, even roughly, I have created a feedback loop that did not exist before. Someone points out the edge case I missed. Someone links the prior work I did not know about. Someone asks a question I cannot answer, which tells me exactly where my understanding stops.

That is the real trade. I give up the comfort of never being seen wrong, and in exchange my mistakes get found in days instead of years.

The artifact also compounds in a way private effort cannot. A solved problem in my head helps me once. The same solution written up helps the next person who hits it, and their follow-up questions sharpen it further. Silent effort evaporates; shared effort accumulates.

The discomfort is real. Publishing something unfinished feels like an invitation to be judged. But unfinished is precisely what makes it correctable; by the time work is polished, I am too invested to hear the feedback that matters.

Start smaller than feels respectable. A note on a problem you just solved. A question you cannot answer yet. The point is not the audience, it is the loop.

I have written separately about [why I write](/blog/category/thoughts/why-i-write): writing as a tool for thinking. This is the other half: what happens when the thinking leaves the drawer.
