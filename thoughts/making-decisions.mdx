Jeff Bezos, in his 2015 letter to Amazon shareholders, split decisions into two types. Type 1 decisions are one-way doors: irreversible, so you should be careful and deliberate. Type 2 decisions are two-way doors: reversible, so you should move fast and correct course if you are wrong.

Most decisions are Type 2. But we treat them all like Type 1.

## The Cost of Slow Decisions

Slow decisions have a hidden cost: they block everything downstream. Your team waits. Momentum dies. The market moves.

A good decision made quickly usually beats a perfect decision made slowly, because you get to learn faster.

## One Question

I do not have a framework for this, just one question: what is the worst that happens if I am wrong?

If the answer is "we can undo it" or "we learn something valuable", decide now. If the answer is genuinely catastrophic, that is a one-way door: slow down and gather more data.

Speed matters. But so does knowing which type of decision you are making.

The same reversibility lens applies to code, which is why I think [technical debt is not always bad](/blog/category/thoughts/technical-debt): a shortcut you can undo is a Type 2 decision wearing an engineering costume.
